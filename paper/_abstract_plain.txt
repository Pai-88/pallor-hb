Conjunctival pallor photographs are an attractive substrate for non-invasive anaemia
screening, and CP-AnemiC — 710 images of Ghanaian children aged 6–59 months with paired
laboratory haemoglobin — has become a convenient public benchmark for the task. We report
that the benchmark contains duplicate records substantial enough to change published
conclusions, and we provide a corrected baseline.

Of the 710 metadata rows only 383 are distinct photographs, in three forms that each defeat
the detection method sufficient for the last. 212 files are byte-identical copies registered
under different identifiers. A further 19 are re-encoded copies — 13 exactly pixel-identical
after region-of-interest extraction, 6 near-identical — which differ at the byte level and so
survive content hashing. The largest and least visible group is 96 shifted or re-cropped
copies of a photograph saved under a different identifier: their bytes differ and their
hand-drawn masks differ, so neither hashing nor bounding-box pixel comparison detects them.
116 groups carry conflicting haemoglobin labels on the same photograph, one spanning
3.3 to 10.4 g/dL, and 125 groups are attributed to more than one hospital — which defeats
site-grouped cross-validation as well as random splits. Evaluating with a random split inflates
AUROC by +0.176 (95% CI +0.120 to +0.232; bootstrap p<0.001,
n_boot = 5000).

After removing all three forms and grouping cross-validation by collection site, twelve
interpretable colour features reach AUROC 0.706 (95% CI 0.653–0.757) for WHO
anaemia (Hb<11 g/dL), against a demographics-only floor of 0.524; the image
contributes +0.182 AUROC (DeLong 95% CI +0.107 to +0.258,
p=2.1 x 10^-6). Bland–Altman limits of agreement span 7.8 g/dL with strong
proportional bias (slope -0.81), so the model is a triage screen and not a haemoglobin
meter. At a threshold catching 90% of anaemic children, specificity falls to 0.303, and
decision curve analysis shows the model is worse than referring every child below a threshold
probability of about 0.26 — so it earns its place only where confirmatory testing is scarce.

We recommend that future work on CP-AnemiC deduplicate under small translations, not merely by
file hash or by aligned pixel comparison, and group cross-validation by site; we release the
detector and the full analysis so that reported numbers can be checked against these controls.
